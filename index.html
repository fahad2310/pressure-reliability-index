      Batters under pressure

      Every high-pressure ball a batter faced is compared with what an average batter produces in the same match state: same over, score, target, wickets and conditions. Points to the right score faster than the situation predicts; points higher up survive longer.

      Bowlers under pressure

      Runs, dot balls and boundaries conceded on high-pressure balls, each set against what an average bowler concedes in the same match state. Wickets taken under pressure proved to be mostly luck (see below), so the index leans on control rather than wickets.

      Where pressure lives

      Pressure here is leverage: how far one delivery can move the result. A win-probability model trained on the data pack scores every ball, and knockout stakes and in-the-moment stress add to it.

      Skill or luck? What repeats under pressure

      The matches were split at random into two halves, forty times over. A real skill shows up in both halves; luck does not. This is why the index rewards control and gives little weight to wickets.

      Boundary and dot-ball control repeat. Split-half reliability of 0.30 to 0.44 means a player's pressure record in one half of the matches predicts the other half.

      Wickets under pressure do not. For bowlers the reliability is 0.02, statistically zero. A bowler who took a lot of pressure wickets last season is not more likely to do it again.

      "Clutch" lift is not measurable. How much better a player is under pressure than in calm moments repeats at below 0.1, so the index gives it only 10% weight.

      How the index is built

      Everything below is computed from the competition data pack. The full methodology, validation and limitations are in the 15-page report.

      Pressure
      Each delivery gets a leverage score: the expected change in the batting side's win probability across everything that can happen on that ball. The win-probability model is gradient boosting with monotonic constraints, cross-validated by match (chases: AUC 0.90, Brier 0.130 against 0.249 for a no-skill baseline). Leverage is multiplied by stakes (knockout ×1.2, final ×1.3) and stress (+10% per wicket in the last 18 balls, up to three; +10% after three dots in a row). The top 25% of deliveries are high pressure.
      Performance
      On every high-pressure ball the player is compared with an average player in the same match state. Batters: runs, dismissals, boundaries and dots. Bowlers: runs conceded, wickets, dots and boundaries.
      Fairness
      Empirical-Bayes shrinkage pulls small samples towards the average. Recent matches count more (three-year half-life). Opposition is weighted: IPL and Full Members 1.0, Zimbabwe and Ireland 0.75, emerging-team events 0.6, Associates 0.5. Eligibility: 120 pressure balls and 10 innings for batters, 150 balls and 10 spells for bowlers, and play since January 2025.
      Index
      0.55 × pressure skill + 0.20 × consistency + 0.15 × win probability added + 0.10 × lift, scaled so 50 is the average eligible player and 10 points is one standard deviation. Consistency is the share of pressure innings or spells won.
      Ranking
      Final rank is the mean of the main rank, the mean rank across seven alternative definitions, and the median rank over sixty bootstrap resamples of matches. Players within one point are ordered by the larger pressure sample. A top-10 place also needs a top-15 rank with every Associate-nation match removed.
